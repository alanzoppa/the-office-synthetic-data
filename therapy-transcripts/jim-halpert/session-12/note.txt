Subjective

Client reported the "holding" is getting heavier — each truth (Pam knowing, Karen knowing) is stacking up. He recognized: "Knowing without acting is its own kind of stuck. I replaced the hovering with the knowing and the knowing is becoming its own hover."

He articulated the full cognitive chain for the first time: "Pam → feelings → wanting → mattering → losing → feeling → being on the ground → being vulnerable → being my father → the chair → disappearing." He recognized the chain as a law he believed without question — "feelings lead to chair, always." But the evidence (design project, father call) breaks the law: "Feelings led to satisfaction, not the chair. The law is wrong."

He described the "yet" evolving: "It used to be 'never.' Then 'someday.' Now it's 'soon.'" He wants to take a step with Pam — not a grand gesture, but a small landing: "I'm not over it." The step will be spontaneous, not manufactured. "The first unperformed moment of my life."

The therapist noted: "Not jumping — stepping." Client adopted this: "The step is the first thing that wouldn't be manufactured."

Assessment

Session 12 is the Jim equivalent of Michael's session 12 (roast) and Pam's session 12 (self-verdict). All three characters articulate their full belief chain and identify where it breaks. Jim's is the most explicitly cognitive — he can write the chain as a logical sequence and identify the exact point where evidence contradicts it.

This is the risk that was identified in session 1 ("he may use insight as a substitute for change"). The client is watching himself do it and naming it.
