# Treatment Plan

**Client:** Jim Halpert
**Date:** Week 3 (Week of October 2, 2006)
**Clinician:** Dr. Sarah Whitfield, LCSW
**Session Type:** Individual Therapy — Treatment Plan Established

## Presenting Problem

Client came to therapy at the suggestion of a coworker (Pam Beesly). Assessment over sessions 1-3 reveals chronic avoidance through detachment and humor. Client describes his life as "hovering" — present but not engaged. He uses humor, "fine," and the "shrug" to prevent things from mattering. He transferred from Scranton to Stamford to escape feelings for Pam, then returned when Stamford closed. The core fear: wanting = mattering = vulnerability to loss. Client has never been in therapy before.

## Relevant History

- **Family:** Father worked a job he hated for 30 years without complaint. Mother was supportive but emphasized being "practical." Client grew up watching his father be stuck and decided he would never be that.
- **Occupational:** Salesman at Dunder Mifflin Scranton. Good at the job but not invested. Transferred to Stamford branch to escape Pam; returned when Stamford closed. Describes being back as "like I never left."
- **Relational:** Has feelings for Pam Beesly (receptionist, engaged to Roy). Dating Karen Filippelli (transferred from Stamford). Describes Karen as "safe" — easy, not requiring vulnerability.
- **Social:** Has friends but no one he'd call in a crisis. Uses humor to maintain distance while appearing connected.

## Mental Status (Session 3)

- **Appearance:** Casual, well-dressed; performed relaxation masking underlying vigilance
- **Behavior:** Cooperative; more engaged than prior sessions; asked genuine question about whether change is possible
- **Mood:** "Terrified and new"
- **Affect:** Defended at baseline; brief vulnerability when discussing change
- **Speech:** Conversational; fewer jokes than intake; more direct
- **Thought process:** Linear; strong self-awareness; recognizes Pam as symbol, not just person
- **Insight:** Limited — knows the pattern but cannot connect insight to behavior change
- **Safety:** Denies SI/HI

## Diagnoses

- **Generalized Anxiety Disorder** (provisional) — anxiety about commitment, engagement, and emotional vulnerability; avoidance as primary coping
- **Rule out: Avoidant Personality Traits** (deferred pending further assessment) — pervasive avoidance of emotional engagement, humor as distance maintenance

## Core Cognitive Schema

**"If I commit to something, I'll be trapped — so it's safer to not care and not choose."**

Origin: Father's 30-year stuckness (commitment = trap) → Client's decision to never be stuck (avoidance of commitment) → Hovering as day-to-day defense (present but not engaged) → Running as emergency protocol (Stamford transfer when Pam got too real)

Current manifestation: Humor as armor; "fine" and "shrug" as micro-exits; hovering above life; running when something matters; dating safe people (Karen) to avoid wanting dangerous people (Pam); "not caring" as posture, not feeling.

## Treatment Goals

| # | Goal | Objectives | Target Session |
|---|------|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | Identify avoidance pattern in real time | Notice the "lift-off" moment; catch the hover, the humor-as-defense, the shrug while it's happening | Ongoing from session 3 |
| 2 | Start caring about one thing | Identify one thing to engage with genuinely; practice being on the ground instead of above it | Sessions 9-13 |
| 3 | Explore humor: connection vs. avoidance | Distinguish humor that shares from humor that hides; track when humor is a wall vs. a window | Sessions 4-8 |
| 4 | Understand the Pam situation | Explore what Pam represents (wanting, mattering, loss); not to resolve but to understand the running | Sessions 12-20 |

## Modality

Psychodynamic with relational emphasis:
- **Pattern tracking:** Identifying repetitive relational and behavioral patterns across sessions
- **Interpretation (timed):** Naming what's happening in the room (including the therapeutic relationship)
- **Silence:** Comfortable with not filling space — allowing the client to sit with discomfort
- **Relational focus:** Using the therapeutic relationship as a mirror for the client's patterns with others

## Frequency

Weekly, 50 minutes

## Review Date

Session 13 — review progress on all four goals, adjust objectives, and determine whether the plan needs revision

## Homework (Session 3 → 4)

Notice the "lift-off" moment — when something starts to matter and he leaves the ground. Just notice the transition. No need to change it. Just see it.
