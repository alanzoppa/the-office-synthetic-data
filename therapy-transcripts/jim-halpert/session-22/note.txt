Date: Week 22
Clinician: Dr. Sarah Whitfield, LCSW
Client: Jim Halpert
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported grief emerging during the week — not about Pam, but about the years. "I was looking at the Pratt certificate page and I thought, 'I could have been doing this for years.' And the grief hit me. A quiet, steady, 'I wasted so much time' sad."

Key grief statements:
- "I was alive for twenty-seven years and I was sleepwalking for most of them."
- "The grief is for the Jim who didn't happen. The Jim who tried. The Jim who applied to design school. The Jim who told Pam before Roy. The Jim who was on the ground."
- "Asleep and safe is my father's chair. And I'd rather have grief than nothing."
- "Grief is the proof that I was here. That I mattered. And mattering is the thing I was afraid of. And I'm not afraid anymore. Not of mattering."

Breakthrough: "I'd rather hurt than be numb. Because numb is the chair. And hurt is the ground. And the ground is where I want to be."

Client declared readiness for the Pam step: "The sentence is 'I'm not not over it.' And I'm going to say it. This week. Not in therapy — out there. To Pam. On the ground." He stated he is "done being numb" and will tell Pam this week.

Objective

- Mood: Grieving; determined; ready

- Speech: Slow; deliberate; the most emotionally direct session of the therapy
- Thought process: Linear; exceptional integration — grief for the lost years, compassion for the afraid kid, determination for the future

Assessment

This is the Jim equivalent of Michael's grief session (22) and Pam's grief-through-painting (22). All three characters grieve for the self that didn't get to exist. Jim's grief is the quietest — "not dramatic, not a breakdown, just sad" — which is consistent with his character (quieter than Michael, less demonstrative than Pam).

"I'd rather hurt than be numb" is the Jim equivalent of Michael's "just is enough" and Pam's "honest is better than nice." Each character's reframe reflects their core: Michael (performance → presence), Pam (suppression → honesty), Jim (avoidance → feeling).

The Pam step declaration ("I'm going to say it. This week.") is the therapeutic endpoint — the client has the sentence, the readiness, and the ethical clearance. The step itself will happen outside therapy.
