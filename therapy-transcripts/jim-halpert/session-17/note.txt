Subjective

Client looked at design programs (Art Institute Philadelphia, community college Scranton, online Pratt certificate) and felt the pull for ten seconds — then shut it down with "be practical." He saw the entire sequence: pull → notice → shutdown → "be practical." He can see every step but cannot yet stop the last one.

The therapist pushed: after 17 weeks of knowing, the client is still hovering, still at Dunder Mifflin, still with Karen, still not telling Pam. The therapist suggested the hovering has become comfortable — familiar — and that "familiar is its own trap" and "you're in the trap you built to avoid his trap."

Client's rupture response:
- Voice went cold
- Explicitly rejected the comparison: "You're saying I'm my father."
- "I built my whole life around not being him and you're telling me it didn't work."
- Terminated the session early: "I need to be somewhere that isn't this room right now."
- Left saying "I'll see you next week. Or I won't."

The interpretation was accurate (the hover has become a familiar trap) but the timing was wrong. The client had just disclosed the design program research and the shutdown reflex — he was vulnerable and the therapist pushed on the most identity-threatening comparison (father) during an open moment.

Assessment

This is the Jim equivalent of Michael's rupture (17) and Pam's rupture (17). All three therapists push on the origin material at the wrong moment. For Jim, the father comparison is the nuclear trigger — his entire identity is organized around "I am not my father." Being told he's in the same chair threatens the core self-structure.

The therapist's interpretation was clinically accurate — the hover has become familiar, and familiarity is its own trap. But the delivery was premature. The client had just been vulnerable (design programs) and the push on the father material was too much, too fast.
