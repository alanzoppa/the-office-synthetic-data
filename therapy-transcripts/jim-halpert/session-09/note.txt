Client: Jim Halpert
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Joke mapping results: ~80% walls, 20% windows. Windows almost exclusively with Pam. Client recognized: "Eighty percent of my humor is avoidance. That's most of me. Most of 'funny Jim' is a wall."

Design project completed and successful — Michael loved it, Dwight called it "adequate." Client reported: "I tried and it worked. And trying and having it work is the most disorienting thing that's happened to me in therapy."

Major insight: "I was avoiding the ground, not the fall. Being on the ground means being in it. Being present. Being invested. Being vulnerable. The fall is a moment. The ground is a state. And I was afraid of the state, not the event."

Breakthrough formulation: "The trap isn't the caring. The trap is the stuckness. And I'm not stuck. I was never stuck. I was afraid of being stuck. And the fear of stuckness kept me hovering." Client connected this to his father: his father was stuck (couldn't leave the job), so caring = trap. But Jim is not stuck — he can leave. So caring does not equal trap for him. The equation is wrong.

Homework: call his father. He's been avoiding the call because hearing his father's voice reminds him of the chair. "He's not the chair. He's my dad. And the chair isn't mine."
