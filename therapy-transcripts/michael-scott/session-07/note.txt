Date: Week 7
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client completed the second behavioral experiment: a no-performance interaction with Jim in the kitchen. He reported Jim's double-take (expecting the usual "bit") and a brief, plain exchange ("Morning, Jim." / "You okay?" / "Yeah. Just morning."). Client described feeling like he "cheated on a test" and passed. Key learning: "Jim doesn't need me to be 'on' to exist in the same room as me."

The session focused on the Dundies as an origin story for manufactured love. Key disclosures:
- Client created the Dundies in his first year as regional manager to "create a family" in an office that lacked culture
- He made himself the host, the center, the "dad" of the family — a role that guarantees necessity
- He purchased the "World's Best Boss" mug for $8 at Spencer's Gifts and lied that Dwight gave it to him
- He explicitly connected this to his father: "I manufactured my own family because the real family I had wasn't — my dad left, my stepdad was a newspaper, my mom was at work"
- He identified his core identity as "the man who stays" — the deliberate inverse of his father: "The worst thing I could be is my father. The man who left. The man who didn't think his son was worth staying for."
- He acknowledged that some employees don't want him there (Toby, Angela, Jim sometimes) but he stays regardless, because leaving = being his father

The clinician planted a seed: "What if some staying is actually staying in a situation that's hurting you?" Client was unable to engage with this yet but accepted it as something to consider.

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed, animated during Dundies discussion, still during the father connection
- Behavior: Cooperative; demonstrated continued capacity for unperformed vulnerability; used sarcasm twice (new pattern, noted since session 6)
- Mood: Energized at start, reflective and emotional during father material
- Affect: Genuine; visible sadness when connecting the mug to his father; no performed affect during the "man who stays" disclosure
- Thought process: Linear; strong integrative capacity — connected Dundies, mug, father, and identity into a coherent narrative
- Insight: Strong — client articulated the full chain: father leaves → I become the opposite → I stay always → I manufacture family to guarantee necessity → I give myself awards because no one else does
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

This session completed a major piece of the attachment exploration (Goal 4). The client's identification of himself as "the man who stays" — and his explicit framing of this as the inverse of his father — is the central identity structure around which the performance pattern is organized. The performance isn't just about being liked; it's about being the kind of person who would never leave, which requires being necessary, which requires being entertaining, which requires manufacturing love.

The "World's Best Boss" mug disclosure (purchased for himself, lied about the source) is clinically significant as a concrete artifact of manufactured self-worth. The client's willingness to disclose the lie represents deepening trust in the therapeutic alliance.

The clinician's seed — "what if some staying is hurting you?" — is planted for the Jan exploration (session 9). The client cannot yet consider that staying might not always be virtuous. This is expected; the belief that staying = goodness is ego-syntonic and protective.

Plan

1. Homework: Continue log and sitting practice. Reflect on the question: "Is manufactured love real love?" Notice the feeling of being "the reason people are together" and whether it feels real or hollow.
2. Next session: Performance vs. connection — differentiating the two conceptually (session 8 in arc)
3. Session 9: Begin Jan exploration using the "staying" frame as entry point
4. The "staying = goodness" belief is now identified as a secondary schema to explore in the Jan context
