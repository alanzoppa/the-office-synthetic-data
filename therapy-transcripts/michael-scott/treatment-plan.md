# Treatment Plan

**Client:** Michael Scott
**Date:** Week 3 (Week of December 4, 2006)
**Clinician:** Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
**Session Type:** Individual Therapy — Treatment Plan Established

## Presenting Problem

Client self-referred for "work stress" and sleep disturbance (waking at approximately 3:00 AM, 4-5 nights per week). Assessment over sessions 1-3 reveals chronic anxiety related to interpersonal approval-seeking, identity fusion with occupational role (regional manager, Dunder Mifflin Scranton), emotional avoidance through humor and performance, and inability to identify personal needs. Client's self-worth is externally contingent — maintained through others' happiness and approval. Sleep disturbance appears to reflect the system's inability to regulate without an audience to perform for.

## Relevant History

- **Father:** Left when client was approximately 4-5 years old. Client has one memory of his father eating cereal; no memory of a goodbye. Father's departure is client's earliest experience of abandonment.
- **Stepfather (Jeff):** Entered client's life around age 7. Physically present but emotionally disengaged — watched TV, did yard work, read the newspaper. When client performed for him at age 8, Jeff responded with "That's nice" and returned to his newspaper. Client did not attempt engagement again.
- **Mother:** Loving but frequently absent due to work. Responded to client's comedy routines and impressions with laughter and attention. Client's performance strategy was reinforced: making mom laugh = earning the right to be present.
- **Occupational:** Strong identification with managerial role. Self-worth appears contingent on being liked by subordinates and approved by corporate authority (David Wallace, CFO). Client gives himself awards (Dundies ceremony, "World's Best Boss" mug purchased for himself).
- **Relational:** Currently in a relationship with Jan (former corporate superior). Jan is described as critical of client's performance at work and personal life. Client frames her criticism as supportive. This warrants further exploration as a pattern of tolerating poor treatment to maintain proximity.

## Mental Status (Session 3)

- **Appearance:** Well-groomed, business casual, attentive
- **Behavior:** Cooperative; completed homework; demonstrated real-time self-observation (consciously chose not to make a joke when receiving the treatment plan)
- **Mood:** "Reflective"
- **Affect:** Mixed — genuine engagement, brief flashes of sadness, managed impulse to perform
- **Thought process:** Linear, insightful; demonstrated capacity to connect patterns across homework entries
- **Insight:** Strengthening — client articulated the core belief explicitly and identified trigger patterns independently
- **Safety:** Denies SI/HI

## Diagnoses

- **Generalized Anxiety Disorder** (provisional) — chronic worry about others' approval, sleep disturbance, hypervigilance about interpersonal cues
- **Rule out: Histrionic personality traits** (deferred pending further assessment) — pervasive pattern of attention-seeking through performance, discomfort when not the center of attention, use of physical appearance and humor to draw attention

## Core Cognitive Schema

**"If I'm not entertaining, people will leave me."**

Origin: Father's departure (people leave without explanation) → Stepfather's disengagement (presence doesn't equal connection; performance doesn't reliably secure engagement from paternal figures) → Mother's responsive engagement (performance = connection; making someone laugh = earning the right to be present)

Current manifestation: Performance as default interpersonal mode; monitoring others' happiness as proxy for self-worth; inability to identify own needs; toleration of criticism in relationships as the price of presence; sleep disturbance as system dysregulation without an audience.

## Treatment Goals

| # | Goal | Objectives | Target Session |
|---|------|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | Increase awareness of performance pattern | Track triggers via Performance Log; identify emotional costs of performance; notice the impulse in real time | Ongoing from session 3 |
| 2 | Develop ability to tolerate being present without performing | Practice stillness in session; behavioral experiments (one interaction per week without leading with humor); track outcomes and discomfort | Sessions 10-13 |
| 3 | Begin identifying personal needs independent of others | Explore values, interests, desires outside of occupational role; distinguish "what I need" from "what they need"; practice self-advocacy in session | Sessions 15-20 |
| 4 | Explore early attachment experiences and connect to current patterns | Process father's departure, stepfather's disengagement, mother's role; connect childhood patterns to Jan relationship; examine how abandonment fear drives current relational choices | Sessions 14-18 |

## Modality

Integrative approach combining:
- **Motivational Interviewing:** Reflection, open questions, honoring ambivalence about change
- **Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy:** Identifying and examining the core belief; tracking cognitive distortions; behavioral experiments
- **Attachment-informed psychodynamic awareness:** Understanding early relational patterns and their current manifestation; tracking transference/countertransference dynamics

## Frequency

Weekly, 50 minutes

## Review Date

Session 13 — review progress on all four goals, adjust objectives, and determine whether the plan needs revision

## Homework (Session 3 → 4)

Continue Performance Log. Add column: note the emotion beneath each performance impulse (anxious, scared, lonely, angry, etc.). The goal is to build the bridge between the trigger and the feeling it covers.
