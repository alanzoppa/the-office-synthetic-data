Date: Week 22
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported that grief has continued to process throughout the week in a manageable, weather-like pattern: "It comes and goes and I let it." Specific moments: seeing a child with his father outside the office (sadness arose, client sat with it); hearing "Landslide" by Fleetwood Mac while making a private mix CD (cried alone at his desk, felt okay afterward).

Key distinction articulated: "Fear says 'nobody will come.' Grief says 'somebody should have come.' And the grief is easier to carry than the fear was. Because the grief is real. The fear was about the future. The grief is about the past. And the past is done."

The session focused on internalization — co-creating coping phrases the client can carry after termination. Five phrases were developed, all originating from the client's own words across prior sessions:

1. "I can be in this room without performing." (from behavioral experiments, sessions 5-12)
2. "The feeling is from 1980-something. The reality is now." (from the radiator/intruder distinction, session 6)
3. "I am not a project. I am a person. And I deserve to be seen." (from the Jan work, sessions 9-15)
4. "Being quiet is not being abandoned." (from the sitting practice, sessions 5-10)
5. "I matter." (from the mug reframe, sessions 16-20)

Client plans to write these on index cards ("the anti-show cards") and keep them in his wallet. He reported: "They feel like your voice. But also my voice." Clinician clarified: all phrases originated from the client; the clinician helped him hear them.

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed; calm; grounded
- Behavior: Cooperative; emotionally present; cried briefly but easily recovered; demonstrated capacity to hold grief and function simultaneously
- Mood: Grounded; warm; appropriately serious about termination approaching
- Affect: Genuine; grief still present but integrated; hopefulness about the coping phrases
- Speech: Natural; measured; humor used lightly as connection
- Thought process: Linear; strong integrative capacity — drawing from 22 weeks of material to synthesize portable tools
- Insight: Strong — client can distinguish between running from feelings and feeling feelings; understands that grief is lighter than avoidance
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

The internalization session represents the transition from therapeutic relationship to self-sufficiency. The five coping phrases are not clinician-prescribed — they are the client's own words, surfaced across the therapy, now organized into a portable format. This is the "internalized steady presence" that the treatment plan (session 20 update) identified as the goal for sessions 23-25.

The grief-weather observation demonstrates that the client can now experience painful affect without it becoming a crisis. He cried at a Fleetwood Mac song and was okay afterward — a stark contrast to session 3, where two minutes of silence at home triggered a phone call to Dwight.

Plan

1. Homework: Write the five coping phrases on index cards. Keep them accessible. Use them when the performance pull, the "nobody's coming" feeling, the "project" feeling, or the silence anxiety arise.
2. Next session: Session 24 — testing vulnerability with someone in his life (sharing something real, not a bit)
3. Final session: Session 25 — termination
4. Note: The client's capacity to hold grief and function simultaneously ("grief as weather") is a marker of therapeutic progress. He no longer needs to convert pain to performance to function.
