Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client arrived with a mix CD he created for the clinician ("Therapy Jams"), demonstrating the pattern identified in session 1 of using gifts and performance to secure interpersonal connection. He reported a busy week centered on integrating employees from the closed Stamford branch (merger), which he framed through a family metaphor ("like when your mom remarries and you get a stepdad"). When the clinician noted the stepdad metaphor, client did not consciously connect it to his own history.

Significant progress was made in exploring the family-of-origin material. Client described:
- Performing comedy routines and impressions for his mother as a child, feeling that making her laugh meant he was "doing something right"
- Attempting to perform for his stepfather Jeff at age 8; Jeff responded with "That's nice" and returned to his newspaper; client did not try again
- His biological father leaving when client was approximately 4-5 years old; client has one memory of his father eating cereal, then nothing
- His mother as loving but working frequently; she "always came home"

When the clinician linked the childhood pattern to the present ("making people happy is how you stay connected"), client initially resisted but then articulated a core belief with striking clarity: "If I'm not making the room work, I'm nobody." When asked what would happen if he were "just there" without performing, client said: "People would leave. They'd get bored, they'd find someone more interesting, and they'd leave."

The clinician named the pattern as a potential internal rule: "If I'm not entertaining, people will leave me." Client confirmed this resonates, adding: "I don't love that it resonates, but yeah, it does."

Client continues to frame the Jan relationship as positive, noting "she hasn't left." This warrants further exploration.

Assessment

Session 2 represents a significant shift from assessment to early insight work. The core cognitive schema — "If I'm not entertaining, people will leave me" — has been identified and confirmed by the client. This belief appears to originate from childhood attachment experiences:

1. Father's departure (age 4-5): Taught that people leave without explanation; connection is not guaranteed
2. Stepfather's emotional disengagement: Taught that presence does not equal connection; performing doesn't reliably secure engagement from paternal figures
3. Mother's responsive engagement to performance: Reinforced that performance = connection; making someone laugh = earning the right to be present

The client's current functioning reflects this early learning: he manufactures connection through humor, gifts, and entertainment; he cannot identify his own needs; he monitors others' happiness as a proxy for his own worth; and he tolerates criticism in relationships (Jan) because at least the person is still present.

The gift of the mix CD is itself clinical data: client is already performing for the clinician, attempting to secure the therapeutic relationship through entertainment. This is expected and is not being challenged at this stage.

Plan

1. Homework assigned: Notice — without changing — when the urge to perform arises. Note what triggered it. Client responded well to this framing and agreed to keep a "Performance Log."
2. Next session: Complete assessment; present formulation and treatment plan
3. Begin exploring Jan relationship more directly in upcoming sessions
4. Continue tracking the "fine" pattern around family material
5. Note for future: The client's idealization of the clinician ("you get me") and gift-giving may intensify; monitor for boundary dynamics
