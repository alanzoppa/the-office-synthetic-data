Date: Week 10
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported continued sitting practice with the "Jan rock" — the feeling that staying with Jan may be self-abandonment. He described it as unchanged but increasingly clear: "I already know the answer. I'm with Jan because I'm afraid of being alone. Not because she makes my life better." He framed Jan's qualification bluntly: "You must be this present. And she's present. So she qualifies." The clinician validated the clarity without pushing toward action.

The session pivoted to the behavioral experiment phase (Goal 2). Client was assigned a full conversation without performing — not the thirty-second windows from previous experiments, but a complete interaction. Client chose Pam as the conversation partner ("she's kind, she doesn't judge, she just receives"). Homework: one full no-performance conversation with Pam, noticing before/during/after.

A notable exchange occurred when client asked whether he's actually changing or "just talking about changing." The clinician enumerated concrete behavioral changes (catching the voice with Jan, standing in silence with Pam, sitting practice, disclosures about the mug/door/list). Client accepted this but noted: "I'm changing on the inside but the outside looks the same."

New insight: client questioned whether his metaphors are part of the performance or genuine cognition. The clinician offered that metaphors may be how he actually thinks, not a performance. Client responded: "Maybe I don't have to kill all the metaphors. Just the ones I use to hide." This is a sophisticated distinction between expressive style (genuine) and defensive packaging (performance).

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed, calmer than earlier sessions
- Behavior: Cooperative; sustained vulnerability; used humor lightly without it functioning as defense
- Mood: Reflective; slightly lighter than session 9
- Affect: Genuine; brief sadness re: Jan; curiosity about the metaphor distinction
- Thought process: Linear; excellent integrative capacity
- Insight: Strong — distinguishing genuine expression from defensive performance
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

The client is transitioning from insight to action. The Jan material has been surfaced and is being held without resolution, which is therapeutically appropriate at this stage. The behavioral experiment with Pam will test whether the unperformed self can sustain a full conversation — a significant step up from thirty-second windows.

The metaphor distinction (genuine cognitive style vs. defensive packaging) is clinically valuable. It gives the client permission to retain his expressive voice while becoming more discriminating about when it serves connection vs. avoidance. This supports Goal 3 (identity beyond performance) — the client is beginning to separate "who I am" from "what I do to protect myself."

2. Next session: Review the Pam conversation results (session 11 — "The Experiment Reveals")
