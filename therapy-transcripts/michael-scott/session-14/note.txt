Date: Week 14
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client arrived reporting unexpected anger that emerged during the week while thinking about his stepfather Jeff and the newspaper incident. This is the first time the client has experienced and expressed anger in therapy. He described it as "hot" in his chest and face, hands tightening, and a "do-something" quality — distinctly different from the sit-with-it feelings (sad, scared, lonely) he's been practicing with.

Key disclosures:
- Client articulated anger at every primary figure: father (for leaving), mother (for not seeing his pain, for marrying Jeff), Jeff (for treating him like furniture), Jan (for the list, for making him feel small), and himself (for still performing at forty-something)
- The central thread: "Nobody sees me. I was right there, the whole time, being funny, being entertaining, and nobody actually saw me."
- Client connected anger to the performance pattern: "The anger became the fuel for the show. I performed harder because I was angry that nobody saw me. But I couldn't show the anger, so I showed the opposite. I showed joy."
- He identified a new rule: "Anger = people leave. If I'm angry, people leave. If I'm funny, people stay."
- The clinician guided him to express the anger plainly (not yelling, just stating). Client delivered a comprehensive anger statement covering all four figures.
- After expressing the anger, it transformed to sadness. Client articulated for the first time: "I deserved better. I was a kid and I deserved better. And I'm still alone. And I deserve to not be alone. I deserve to be seen."

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed; visibly emotional from early in session
- Behavior: Cooperative; requested a pause mid-session to sit with feelings ("Can I just sit here for a minute?"); first time requesting space for affect rather than filling it
- Mood: "Exhausted. Good exhausted."
- Affect: Genuine anger (first expression in therapy); transition to deep sadness; tearfulness; no performed affect throughout
- Speech: Slowed; deliberate; anger expressed in clear, direct statements without tangential storytelling
- Thought process: Linear; strong integrative capacity — connected anger to performance, to the show, to the swallowing pattern
- Insight: Strong — client articulated that anger is "self-worth trying to speak" (clinician's framing, accepted and integrated); recognized he deserved better as a child
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

This session is a major emotional breakthrough. The client accessed anger — a previously forbidden affect — and expressed it directly without converting it to performance. The formulation "the show is swallowed anger, turned inside out" is a clinical milestone that reframes the entire performance pattern as an anger suppression mechanism.

The client's statement "I deserved better" is the first expression of self-worth independent of external validation. This is foundational for Goal 3 (personal needs/identity) and represents a shift from understanding his worth through others' approval to claiming it internally.

The request to sit in silence ("Can I just sit here for a minute?") is behaviorally significant: the client who couldn't tolerate two minutes of silence at home (session 3, Performance Log) now actively seeks silence to process affect.

Plan

1. Homework: No log, no experiments this week. Just sit with what came up. If anger returns, let it be there without swallowing or performing.
2. Next session: Session 15 — shame about needing therapy (arc beat). The anger work may surface shame about vulnerability.
3. Note: The anger expression opens the door to the Jan work — anger at Jan was expressed but not fully explored. This will come in sessions 17-21.
4. Note: The "I deserved better" statement should be reinforced in future sessions. It is the seeds of self-advocacy.
