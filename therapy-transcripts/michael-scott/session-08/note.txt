Date: Week 8
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported continued reflection on the "manufactured love" question from session 7. He arrived at a nuanced answer: the Dundies are both real and manufactured. The awards and people's enjoyment are real, but the purpose underneath — being the center, being necessary, being celebrated — is manufactured. He compared it to learning how a magic trick works: "The trick is still cool, but you can't unsee the mechanism."

The session introduced the core therapeutic distinction between performing and connecting. Client articulated it clearly: "Performing at someone keeps them at a distance. They're watching the show. They're not seeing me. They're seeing the standee." He recognized that the break room and kitchen moments were connection (small, quiet, no show) while the Dundies are performance (big, loud, controlled).

He raised a critical fear: "What if the real Michael, without the bits and the impressions and the Dundies, is just boring?" When asked to describe that "boring" version of himself, he said: "He's lonely. And he's scared. And he wants connection. He just doesn't know how to get it without the show. That's not boring. That's just human." This is the first time the client has framed his unperformed self in positive (or even neutral) terms.

When asked about applying the performing vs. connecting distinction to Jan, client said he "can't imagine" being with Jan without performing: "I'm always managing her. I'm always trying to stay ahead of the next criticism." He described hiding vulnerability from Jan to avoid giving her "ammunition." He framed the relationship as not painful but acknowledged feeling "small" in it.

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed, calm demeanor at entry

- Mood: Reflective; "good tired" at end
- Affect: Genuine; brief sadness when discussing Jan; notable warmth when arriving at "just human"
- Thought process: Linear; strong integrative capacity — connecting performing/connecting distinction to Dundies, to Jan, to the standee metaphor
- Insight: Strong — client can now distinguish between performing and connecting, articulate the fear underneath (being boring/nothing without the show), and reframe the unperformed self as "human" rather than "nobody"
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

Session 8 marks a meaningful shift in the client's self-concept. The binary he has lived with — "either I'm the show or I'm nobody" — is beginning to crack. He identified a third option ("just human") and was able to describe his unperformed self with compassion rather than contempt. This is foundational for Goal 3 (identifying personal needs) and Goal 2 (tolerating presence without performing).

The Jan material is now clearly framed as a performance relationship: client manages her, hides vulnerability, and frames criticism-avoidance as the price of her presence. The "ammunition" metaphor is significant — it frames intimacy as dangerous in this relationship. The Jan exploration is formally scheduled for session 9.

Plan

1. Homework: Continue log and sitting practice. New reflection: sit with the feeling of being "just human" (contrasted with "nobody's coming"). Notice the performing vs. connecting distinction in daily interactions.
2. Next session: Jan exploration — the relationship as a case study in "staying" vs. connecting (Goal 4)
3. The "just human" reframe is a clinical asset to reinforce in future sessions
4. Note: The client's growing capacity for self-compassion ("that's not boring, that's just human") is the most significant development since the core belief was identified in session 2
