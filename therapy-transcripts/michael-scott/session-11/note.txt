Date: Week 11
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client completed the full no-performance conversation with Pam. The conversation lasted approximately five minutes and focused on Pam's art background — a topic client had never asked about. Key moments:
- Client asked "Do you still draw?" — Pam responded with surprise and openness
- Pam disclosed vulnerability: "I'm not sure I'm good enough to do it seriously"
- Client responded authentically: "I think you're good enough" — Pam said "I know you do"
- Client reported a "warm" feeling in his chest that lasted approximately an hour afterward

Client's before/during/after report:
- Before: Usual panic ("I need to be entertaining, she's going to be bored")
- During: "Calm. Like I was actually there. With her. Not performing at her."
- After: "Warm. Like something warm in my chest. Not the rock. The rock is different."

Client articulated a critical insight: "The show is about me. Connection is about us. And I've been doing the show my whole life because the show is safe. I control the show. I can't control connection." He recognized that he "showed up first" — dropped the show and Pam met him there.

New metaphor distinction: fireworks vs. candles. "The firework is big and loud and everyone looks, but it's over in five seconds. The candle is small and quiet but it lasts." The clinician noted this metaphor is being used to understand, not to hide — reinforcing the session 10 distinction between metaphors as windows vs. shields.

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed; visibly lighter than recent sessions
- Behavior: Cooperative; animated when describing the conversation but without performed quality; genuine enthusiasm
- Mood: "Warm" (client's word)
- Affect: Genuine warmth; hopefulness; pride without grandiosity
- Speech: Natural pace; metaphors integrated organically rather than as deflection
- Thought process: Linear; strong integrative capacity; connecting the Pam conversation to the performing/connecting distinction from session 8
- Insight: Strong — client can now articulate the difference between performance and connection from lived experience, not just conceptually
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

This session represents the first unambiguous disconfirmation of the core belief ("If I'm not entertaining, people will leave me"). The client had a full, real conversation without performing and the outcome was positive — not just "not bad" but genuinely connecting. The Pam interaction produced an emotional experience (warmth, being seen) that the performance pattern has never produced. This is experiential evidence that the client can use to challenge the schema.

The "show is about me, connection is about us" formulation is a clinical milestone. It reframes the performance pattern as self-focused rather than other-focused — which the client had never recognized. He believed he was being generous (entertaining people, making them happy) and is beginning to see that the performance is actually self-protective.
