Date: Week 22
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client completed the crumbs vs. candles observation: most relationships are crumbs (Dwight = loyalty without seeing, Angela = crumbs, Kevin = funny crumbs, Dundies = "crumbs dressed up as candles"). Candles: Pam, Oscar, the therapeutic relationship, night walks, private mix CDs.

The session focused on grief work. The clinician guided the client to imagine the four-year-old sitting by the door and speak to him directly. Client delivered a grief statement:
- To his younger self: "I'm sorry nobody came. I'm sorry you sat there and waited. It wasn't your fault. You were four."
- About Jeff: "I'm sorry there was a man in your house who didn't see you. You didn't do anything wrong."
- About his mother: "I'm sorry she didn't see you either. She loved you, but she didn't see you sitting by the door. And you deserved to be seen."
- To his younger self: "I see you. You matter. You don't have to be funny. You're enough. Just the kid in the pajamas by the door. You're enough."

Client cried openly in session for the first time. He reported: "I've never let myself feel this. I always turned it into something — a joke, a metaphor, an insight. I never just let it be pain." He described the experience as "a candle that's been waiting to be lit for thirty years. And it just got lit. And it's warm. And it's not going to go out." He also reported relief: "Like I've been holding a breath for thirty years and I just exhaled."

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed; became disheveled during crying
- Behavior: Cooperative; cried openly; did not deflect to humor or metaphor during the grief expression; requested no homework ("I just need to be with this")
- Mood: Moved from analytical to deeply grieving to relief
- Affect: Raw grief; open tearfulness; no performed affect whatsoever; relief after expression
- Speech: Slowed; broken by emotion during the grief statement; no tangential storytelling
- Thought process: Present-focused; affective rather than cognitive
- Insight: Deepened from cognitive understanding to embodied experience of grief
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

This is the grief session — the emotional core of the therapy arc. The client accessed and expressed grief for the childhood he didn't get: not the anger (session 14), not the understanding (sessions 5-7), but the raw pain of a child who was not seen. The self-directed compassion ("I see you. You matter. You're enough.") is the culmination of the self-worth development that began with "I deserved better" (session 14) and progressed through the mug reframe (session 16) and "just is enough" (session 20).

The client's ability to stay with the grief without converting it to metaphor, humor, or insight is unprecedented. He has been building toward this capacity for 22 sessions — the sitting practice, the anger work, the behavioral experiments, the rupture repair — all were scaffolding for this moment.

The "candle that's been waiting to be lit" is both a metaphor and a felt experience. Notably, the client used a metaphor here, but it emerged from the affect rather than packaging it — confirming the session 19 distinction between metaphors as windows vs. shields.

Plan

1. No homework assigned. Client requested time to be with the grief.
2. Next session: Session 23 — internalizing a different voice (co-creating coping phrases). This transitions from processing to consolidation.
3. The grief work is not complete — client noted "this isn't a one-session thing" — but the initial expression has occurred. The grief will continue to process in the background.
4. Note: The self-compassion expressed toward the four-year-old ("I see you. You matter. You're enough.") is the internalized version of the therapeutic relationship. The client is beginning to carry the clinician's function inside himself.
