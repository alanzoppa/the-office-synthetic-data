Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy (Treatment Plan Review)

Subjective

Midpoint review session. Client and clinician reviewed all four treatment goals:

**Goal 1 (Awareness of performance pattern):** Client reports real-time awareness — can catch the performance as it happens (e.g., the "charming boyfriend voice" with Jan, the Santa bit during the budget meeting). Cannot always stop it, but sees it. Clinician assesses: significant shift from no awareness to real-time awareness. Goal considered substantially achieved; will continue informally.

**Goal 2 (Tolerate being present without performing):** Client has completed four behavioral experiments (Pam x3, Oscar x1), all successful. Sitting practice for "nobody's coming" feeling established and effective. Client describes it as "building the muscle." Clinician assesses: ahead of schedule. Revised goal: raise the bar from low-stakes to medium-stakes situations (staff meetings, conversations with authority figures).

**Goal 3 (Identify personal needs/identity):** Client has identified curiosity and warmth as genuine (non-performed) traits. Can describe the "real Michael" beyond the show. But cannot yet identify personal needs or desires outside of work/performing. "I don't know what I enjoy outside of work. I eat cereal for dinner. That's not a life." Clinician assesses: early stage. Revised goal: shift from exploration to active building — try activities outside work that don't involve performing. Client mentioned music/mix CDs as a potential interest.

**Goal 4 (Early attachment exploration):** Client accessed and connected the foundational memories (father leaving, sitting by the door, Jeff's newspaper, mother's responsive engagement to performance). Identified the "man who stays" identity. Connected Jan relationship to the waiting pattern. Clinician assesses: understanding is strong, behavioral change has not yet occurred. "I understand the pattern, but the pattern is still running." Revised goal: focus specifically on the Jan question — not to decide, but to understand what he's choosing and what he's giving up.

Client expressed fear about the Jan question: "I can't go back to the door. I can't sit by the door again." Clinician reflected that he is not the four-year-old anymore — he has tools, awareness, and people. Client was able to hear this.

New cognitive shift: client identified the "right move/wrong move" belief — "If I do the wrong thing, the audience leaves." Clinician reframed: "What if there's no wrong move? Just life." Client responded: "Life isn't a performance. I've been running my life like it's a performance with a right way and a wrong way."
