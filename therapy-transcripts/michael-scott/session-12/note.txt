Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client completed both homework conversations. Pam conversation continued smoothly (asked about a watercolor on her desk; Pam shared it was from college). Oscar conversation was a breakthrough — client asked about weekend plans, Oscar shared his interest in documentary films, and they talked for ten minutes. Client noted: "I've worked with Oscar for years and I never knew that about him. Because I was always too busy performing to ask him about himself."

Key insights from the Oscar conversation:
- Client identified his fear: "With Oscar I have this fear that he thinks I'm dumb. The show is how I compensate. 'I may not be smart, but I'm funny.'"
- The predicted outcome (Oscar thinks he's dumb) did not occur. Oscar simply responded to genuine curiosity.
- Client articulated: "The show lied to me. The show said I need it or Oscar will think I'm dumb. Turns out Oscar doesn't think about that at all."

New identity development: Client identified curiosity as a genuine (non-performed) trait. "The curiosity is just me. That's not part of the show. The show is jokes and bits. The curiosity is just... me." He expanded the "real Michael" beyond the scared/lonely version: "The real Michael is curious. And warm. And kind of scared. And lonely sometimes. But also curious and warm."

The session introduced the "performing acceptance of pain" pattern. Client disclosed a recent office roast where colleagues (including Jim) made jokes at his expense. He laughed along despite being hurt. Key disclosure: "Jim said I was 'emotionally exhausting.' And everybody laughed. And I laughed too. But inside I was thinking, 'That's true. I am needy. I am exhausting.'" He recognized this as performing being okay with being hurt because "the boss who can't take a joke is no fun. And the boss who is no fun gets left."

The clinician noted that client says "ow" in therapy every week, and the room doesn't empty. Client was asked to consider saying "ow" to one person outside therapy.

Assessment

The client has now completed four behavioral experiments (Pam x3, Oscar x1), all successful. The core belief is being systematically disconfirmed through lived experience. The client's self-concept is expanding: from "the show or nothing" (session 4) to "the show or the scared/lonely guy" (session 8) to "the show or a curious, warm, sometimes-scared person" (session 12). This is meaningful progress on Goal 3 (identifying personal needs/identity).

The roast disclosure introduces a new pattern: performing acceptance of pain. This is a variation of the performance defense — instead of performing to secure connection, the client performs to prevent disconnection when hurt. The two patterns share the same core: vulnerability = danger.

Session 13 is the treatment plan review. Goals 1 and 2 are on track or ahead. Goal 4 (attachment) is ahead of schedule (the father/door memory and Jan exploration occurred earlier than planned). Goal 3 (personal needs/identity) is emerging organically through the behavioral experiments.
