Date: Week 4
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client arrived having completed the Performance Log with the added feelings column. He reported that the noticing is getting easier ("I can almost catch it before it happens") but naming the feeling underneath remains difficult. Key entries:

1. Tuesday staff meeting: Performed a Santa-as-regional-manager bit during a budget meeting. Feeling underneath: "scared" — David Wallace had sent a cost-cutting memo and client feared being seen as incompetent if the meeting was boring.
2. Thursday lunch: Performed a "food critic" routine when Dwight joined him in his office. Feeling underneath: "lonely" — client was eating alone and Dwight's arrival triggered the performance instead of connection.

Client demonstrated significant new insight: "The funny is not the point. The funny is the defense." He articulated that humor replaces the feeling rather than expressing it.

A pivotal moment occurred when client used a "standee" metaphor (a cardboard cutout of himself that people see while the real person is hidden behind it). The clinician named this as a "performance of vulnerability" — packaging insight into an entertaining metaphor. Client recognized this in real time and was able to restate the same content without the metaphorical packaging: "Nobody knows me. The real me. People know the funny version... the parts that are scared and lonely and wanting, those parts don't get to come out."

Notable: Client did not perform for the back half of the session and noted this himself at the end.

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed, less animated than previous sessions
- Behavior: Cooperative; caught his own performance of vulnerability in real time; able to sit with plain affective statements
- Mood: "Weird" (client's word) — clarified as unfamiliar, not unpleasant
- Affect: More genuine than any prior session; visible sadness when speaking without metaphor; no deflection to humor in the final 15 minutes
- Speech: Variable — rapid during entries and metaphor, notably slowed during the unpackaged vulnerability
- Thought process: Linear; strong insight; demonstrated metacognitive awareness (watching himself perform)
- Insight: Strengthening significantly — client can now identify the defense in real time and articulate the difference between performed and genuine self-disclosure
- Judgment: Intact
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

Session 4 marks the transition from pure awareness (Goal 1) to early behavioral experimentation (Goal 2). The client's capacity to catch his own "performance of vulnerability" — the standee metaphor being entertaining even while describing pain — is a meaningful clinical development. He can now distinguish between genuine self-disclosure and packaged self-disclosure.

The "lonely" entry is the first time the client has named a feeling that isn't fear-based. The loneliness at lunch, alone in his office, connects to the attachment thread (Goal 4): the boy who performed for his mother's attention is the man performing for Dwight's attention at lunch. The function is identical — connection through entertainment.

Plan

1. Homework: Continue Performance Log. Add one behavioral experiment — one low-stakes conversation this week without leading with humor or a bit. Just see what happens. The goal is to test the prediction that people leave when the performance stops.
2. Next session: Review the experiment; begin exploring the stepfather material more directly (Goal 4)
3. Treatment plan goals 1 and 2 are now both active
4. Note: Client's growing capacity for unperformed vulnerability is encouraging but fragile — continue to validate without over-celebrating, which could trigger performance
