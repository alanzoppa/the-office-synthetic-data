Date: Week 24
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported using the coping cards during the week. Card #3 ("I am not a project. I am a person. And I deserve to be seen") was used during a phone call with Jan, who added a new item to her list (dress more professionally). Client touched the card in his wallet and said, "I hear you. I'm not going to change how I dress right now." Jan said "okay" and moved on. Client did not perform. Key reflection: "The show didn't have to run. And everything was fine."

The session focused on the vulnerability test — the final behavioral task. Client identified what he wants to share: telling Pam that therapy has changed his life. "Not the whole story. Just — 'Pam, I've been going through something, and it's been really important, and I just wanted to tell you because you're one of the people who made a difference.'" Client articulated the fear (Pam might be weird, might polite-smile, might not receive it as real) and then made a critical cognitive shift: "The saying it is the thing. Not the response. I've been basing everything on the response. But the being real is the point, whether or not the other person receives it."

When asked about termination (next session is the last), client reported: "Scared. Sad. Grateful. All of it. I don't want it to end. But I also feel ready. Not 'fixed' ready. 'Ready to carry the work without this room' ready."

Key statement: "Me is lighter than the show. Before, I was carrying the show and the fear and the swallowed anger and the 'nobody's coming' and the mug and the performance. That was heavier. This is lighter. This is just me."

Client offered a pre-emptive goodbye: "Thank you. For being the candle. For being the room where the show turned off. For seeing me."

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed; calm; grounded
- Behavior: Cooperative; emotionally present; demonstrated mature capacity to anticipate loss while functioning
- Mood: Mixed — scared, sad, grateful (all named accurately)
- Affect: Genuine warmth; anticipatory grief about termination; no performed affect
- Speech: Natural; unhurried; humor absent (appropriate for the content)
- Thought process: Linear; integrative; forward-looking
- Insight: Strong — client has internalized the therapeutic function and can articulate readiness to carry it independently
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

The pre-termination session confirms the client's readiness for ending. The cognitive shift — "the being real is the point, not the response" — represents the complete inversion of the core belief that brought him into therapy. He entered believing "if I'm not entertaining, people will leave." He's leaving believing "the showing up is enough, even if nobody claps."

The Jan boundary-setting (declining to change his dress, without performance) is the most significant behavioral evidence of change. The relationship that most intensely triggers the performance pattern now has a counter-example: he said no, plainly, and the world didn't end.

The "me is lighter than the show" formulation is the final reframe of the identity work (Goal 3). The client can now articulate that authenticity is less burdensome than performance — the opposite of his starting position.

Plan

1. Homework: Tell Pam. Share something real. "Therapy has changed my life and you're one of the people who made a difference."
2. Next session: Session 25 — termination. Review the arc. Say goodbye without a show.
3. Note: The pre-emptive thank-you ("for being the candle, for seeing me") suggests the client may be emotional during termination. Allow space for this.
4. Note: The Jan relationship has not been resolved. The client is aware of the pattern, has set one boundary, and holds the question. This is the appropriate state at termination — the work continues beyond the therapy room.
