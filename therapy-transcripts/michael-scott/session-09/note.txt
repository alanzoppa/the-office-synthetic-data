Date: Week 9
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client arrived having watched himself perform with Jan throughout the week — a significant development in real-time self-observation. He reported a dinner Saturday where he caught himself using a "charming boyfriend voice" (deeper version of his regular voice). Jan called him on it: "You're doing a voice." When he stopped, he listened to her for ten minutes without performing. Jan's response: "It's weird." She then became distant, warming up only when he resumed performing.

Key Jan disclosures during this session:
- Jan has a literal written list of things she wants him to change about himself (12 items including "be less needy," "stop doing impressions in professional settings," "dress more professionally")
- Client took the list home, looked at it alone, and thought: "If I fix all these things, will she love me?" He doesn't know the answer
- Client articulated: "She doesn't want the real me. She wants the show. She doesn't want the cereal-for-dinner guy."
- He named the parallel: "It sounds like the door. Sitting by the door waiting for somebody who's not coming. Except I'm waiting for Jan to show up. To actually show up. And she's not coming."
- When the clinician asked why he's with Jan, his answer was entirely about avoiding aloneness, not about Jan specifically: "Because she's here. Because she didn't leave. Because I'm not alone."

The clinician introduced the seed from session 7 in concrete form: "What if staying with Jan is the same as your father leaving — not in the obvious way, but in the sense that you're abandoning yourself by staying?" Client reported a physical sensation ("a rock in my chest") but agreed to sit with it.

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed; visibly more serious than typical
- Behavior: Cooperative; sustained vulnerability throughout; no performance deflection during the Jan material
- Mood: "Terrible but clear" (client's words)
- Affect: Genuine sadness; frustration; brief flash of anger when describing the list; no performed affect
- Speech: Measured; slower than any prior session; no tangential storytelling
- Thought process: Linear; strong integrative capacity — connected Jan relationship to the door/waiting pattern independently
- Insight: Strong — client can now articulate that the Jan relationship replays the childhood abandonment dynamic, and that "staying" may be a form of self-abandonment
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

This session is a major clinical development. The client has identified the Jan relationship as a replay of the core attachment wound: waiting for someone to "actually show up" while performing to keep them in the room. The "list" disclosure is particularly concerning — it indicates a pattern of systematic criticism that the client has been tolerating and internalizing.

The clinician's framing — "staying with Jan might be abandoning yourself" — directly challenges the client's identity as "the man who stays" (established in session 7). This creates productive cognitive dissonance. The client's physical response ("rock in my chest") suggests the framing landed at a somatic level.

No directive was given regarding the relationship. The client is not ready for action; he is in the awareness phase of this particular thread.

Plan

1. Homework: Continue log and sitting practice. New practice: sit with the "Jan rock" — the feeling that staying with Jan may be self-abandonment. No decisions, no action. Just noticing the feeling.
2. Next session: First behavioral experiment outside therapy — assigned in session 10, the next arc beat
3. The Jan material will continue to unfold across sessions 10-12 as the client processes the "staying vs. self-abandonment" question
4. Note: Client's real-time self-observation (catching the "charming boyfriend voice" during dinner) demonstrates that the awareness work (Goal 1) is generalizing from session to daily life
5. Note: The Jan "list" is clinically significant and should be revisited in the context of the treatment plan review at session 13
