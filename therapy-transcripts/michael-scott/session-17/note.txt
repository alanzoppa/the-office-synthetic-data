Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported that the mug homework ("I matter") progressed across the week — from feeling "stupid" on Monday to "true-ish" by Friday. This is consistent with the pattern of repeated exposure reducing the intensity of the cognitive dissonance.

The session shifted to the clinician's interpretation that the performance pattern serves a dual function: (1) keeping people in the room (the established formulation), and (2) keeping people at a distance — reproducing Jeff's emotional disengagement as a protective strategy against genuine intimacy. The interpretation was: "The performance keeps everyone at exactly the distance your stepfather was at — present, but not engaged. Close enough to see, too far to know."

Client response was a significant rupture:
- Voice went flat; body language closed
- Explicitly rejected the comparison to Jeff: "I'm nothing like Jeff. Jeff didn't care. I care too much."
- Framed the interpretation as being "made the bad guy" and accused the clinician of "turning this around"
- Listed his therapeutic work as evidence that he's not hiding: "I've been honest. I've been vulnerable. I told you about the door. I told you about the mug."
- Reported feeling like the clinician "moved the goalposts" — that nothing he does is ever enough
- Made the critical comparison: "That's exactly how Jan makes me feel. Like I'm a project that's never finished. And I didn't expect to feel that here."
- Terminated the session early: "I think we're done for today."
- Left saying "I'll see you next week. Maybe."

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed at entry; visibly shut down during rupture
- Behavior: Cooperative initially; shifted to cold, defended, and then dismissive; terminated session early (first time); left the room
- Mood: Shifted from open to "cold" to angry to hurt
- Affect: Flat during the rejection; flash of hurt at the door when comparing clinician to Jan
- Speech: Became clipped and flat; lost the natural warmth of recent sessions
- Thought process: Rigid during rupture; the Jan comparison was insightful despite the distress
- Insight: Compromised during rupture, but the Jan comparison demonstrates the transference is partially visible to the client
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

This is the first major therapeutic rupture. The clinician's interpretation — connecting the performance pattern to Jeff's distance — was clinically accurate but poorly timed. The client had just reported progress on the mug homework and was in a relatively open state. The interpretation bypassed the client's readiness and triggered two simultaneous responses:

1. **Identity threat:** The comparison to Jeff threatens the client's core identity as "the man who stays" / "the man who tries" (established session 7). Jeff is the anti-identity. Being compared to Jeff is ego-dystonic in the extreme.

2. **Transference:** The client experienced the interpretation as the same dynamic he experiences with Jan — being a "project that's never finished," where nothing he does is ever enough. This is clinically significant: the client is experiencing the clinician as Jan, not as Jeff. The comparison to Jan was made explicitly and spontaneously.

The rupture is repairable. The client said "I'll see you next week. Maybe" — not "I'm never coming back." The door is open. The clinician's acknowledgment ("I pushed too fast") was appropriate but insufficient; the repair will need to be the focus of session 18.

Plan

1. No homework assigned (client left before homework was given)
2. Next session: Rupture repair. The clinician must own the timing error, validate the client's experience of feeling like a "project," and address the Jan transference directly. The interpretation itself was not wrong, but it was premature.
3. The rupture is clinically valuable: it demonstrates that the client can identify when he's being treated like Jan treats him, which is evidence of growing relational awareness. The client's parting statement ("I didn't expect to feel that here") shows he holds the therapeutic relationship to a different standard than other relationships.
4. Note: If the client does not return for session 18, outreach is warranted. However, "maybe" suggests likely return.
