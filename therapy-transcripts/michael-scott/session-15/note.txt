Date: Week 15
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported two successful anger experiences during the week:
1. Wednesday: Dwight made a comment about client not being a "real manager." Client felt the anger (hot in chest), did not convert to a joke, stood with it, and it passed like a wave. First instance of anger tolerance without performance in a live interaction.
2. Friday: Jan called with criticism. Instead of performing/deflecting, client said "Jan, I hear what you're saying. I need to think about it." Jan did not escalate. Client reports feeling "powerful" — a word he has never used for himself.

Client then disclosed shame about being in therapy. Key themes:
- Believes therapy = proof he's broken; "If I was strong enough, I wouldn't need help"
- Measured himself against Jeff as the model of strength — then recognized this as measuring himself against emotional disengagement: "What Jeff did was check out. What Jeff did was not care. That's not strength."
- Clinician asked what he'd say if Pam wanted to see a therapist. Client: "I'd say that's brave. You deserve support." Recognized: "I give Pam the compassion I won't give myself."
- New insight: "I have a giving muscle but no receiving muscle." He earns everything (mom's attention, employees' goodwill, Jan's presence, the mug) and doesn't know how to receive without earning.
- Fear: receiving without earning feels like a loan that could be revoked. "If I didn't perform for it, they could realize they made a mistake and take it back."
- Clinician reflected: 14 weeks of unperformed sessions, no performance, and the therapist is still here. "You didn't earn it. You just showed up. And that was enough."
- Client: "Showing up has never been enough. I've always had to show up AND be funny."

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed; calmer at entry; became animated during the shame disclosure
- Behavior: Cooperative; genuine; used the Pam reflection to access self-compassion indirectly
- Mood: Moved from proud (anger success) to ashamed (therapy stigma) to reflective (receiving insight)
- Affect: Genuine; shame visible in posture and voice; relief when the "just showing up is enough" reframe landed
- Speech: Slowed during shame material; accelerated when describing anger successes
- Thought process: Linear; strong integrative capacity — connected shame to Jeff, to giving/receiving, to the mug
- Insight: Strong — identified the giving/receiving imbalance and the "receiving without earning = revocable loan" belief
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

The shame disclosure is a critical clinical development. The client's belief that needing help = weakness maps directly onto his stepfather model (emotional disengagement as strength). His ability to identify this — "I'm calling what Jeff did strength, and what Jeff did was check out" — demonstrates significant cognitive flexibility.

The "giving muscle / no receiving muscle" formulation is a precise articulation of the core interpersonal dynamic. The client has spent his life earning connection and cannot tolerate receiving it freely because unearned connection feels insecure (revocable). The clinician's reflection — "you just showed up and that was enough" — challenges this directly with 14 weeks of therapeutic evidence.

The anger successes are behaviorally significant: the client tolerated anger in two live interactions (Dwight, Jan) without converting to performance. The Jan interaction is particularly important — he set a boundary ("I need to think about it") without performing, and Jan did not escalate or leave.

Plan

1. Homework: Continue anger tolerance practice. New: receive one compliment/kindness without deflecting or performing. Just say "thank you" and let it land.
2. Next session: Session 16 — the "World's Best Boss" mug as symbol (arc beat). Connect the mug to the giving/receiving and earning/revocable themes.
3. The Jan boundary success is a prototype for future boundary-setting work
4. Note: The "just showing up is enough" reframe is the therapeutic answer to "nobody's coming" — the client is beginning to internalize that presence without performance is sufficient
