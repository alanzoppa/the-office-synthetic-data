Date: Week 5
Clinician: Dr. Karen Mitchell, LCSW
Client: Michael Scott
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client completed both homework assignments: the Performance Log with feelings column and the behavioral experiment (one conversation without performing).

Behavioral experiment report: Client stood in the break room with Pam without leading with a joke or bit. He said "Hey, Pam" and got his coffee in silence. He reported intense internal panic during the silence but Pam responded normally — asked if he was okay, said he looked tired, they exchanged a few plain words. Client reported: "She didn't leave. She just talked to me like a person." He described the experience as "good weird."

Performance Log entries:
1. Monday: Did a DVD commentary over a corporate safety video. Feeling underneath: "inadequate" — he believed he had to "be the value" or the meeting wasn't worth attending. Key statement: "If I'm not entertaining, I'm not necessary."
2. Thursday: Continued pattern of performing in staff interactions. Feeling underneath: "lonely."

The session deepened significantly when client connected the "nobody's coming" feeling to a specific childhood memory he had not previously disclosed: after his father left, he would sit by the front door waiting for his father to return. His mother would find him there and say "Daddy's not coming, honey." Client would then go to his room and practice impressions — because making his mother laugh meant someone was present. He identified this as the origin of the performance strategy.

Objective

- Appearance: Well-groomed; less animated at entry, became still during the childhood memory
- Behavior: Cooperative; visibly emotional during the door memory; did not deflect to humor
- Mood: "Good tired" (client's words at end of session)
- Affect: Genuine; tearfulness when discussing the door memory; no performed affect
- Speech: Slowed significantly during the memory; no tangential storytelling
- Thought process: Linear; connected childhood experience to current pattern without prompting
- Insight: Strong — client articulated the full chain: father leaves → waiting → nobody comes → perform for mother → mother is present → performance = connection
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

This session represents a clinical breakthrough. The client accessed and articulated the foundational attachment memory (sitting by the door, waiting for his father) without prompting, and connected it independently to the performance strategy. This addresses Goal 4 (early attachment exploration) earlier than anticipated — the client's readiness exceeded the planned timeline.

The behavioral experiment (break room with Pam) was successful: the predicted catastrophe (Pam leaving, being bored, something being wrong) did not occur. This is the first real-world disconfirmation of the core belief "If I'm not entertaining, people will leave me." The client registered this as significant but small — which is therapeutically useful, as over-interpreting it could trigger performance.

The "nobody's coming" feeling is the affective core beneath the performance schema. The client can now name it. The next phase of work involves building tolerance for this feeling without fleeing into performance.

Plan

1. Homework: Continue Performance Log. New practice: when the "nobody's coming" feeling arises in silence, sit with it for one minute before reaching for phone, TV, or a bit. Just notice and tolerate.
2. Next session: Continue mapping triggers (Goal 1); begin building a systematic trigger map for the approval-seeking pattern
3. The stepfather material (Goal 4) has been partially accessed through the father/door memory; continue to let it emerge organically rather than directing
4. Note: The break room experiment with Pam is a useful prototype — consider assigning additional low-stakes experiments as the client's tolerance grows
