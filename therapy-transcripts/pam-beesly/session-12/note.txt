Date: Week 12
Clinician: Dr. James Pierce, PsyD
Client: Pam Beesly
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported continued nice-naming awareness. She caught herself about to tell Jim "Roy's fine" and let the real word — "distant" — exist in her mind. She identified the distinction: "'Fine' is a wall. 'Distant' is a window. And descriptions give me something to work with."

The session focused on Jim. Client disclosed that wanting Jim to see her is "the most dangerous want I have" because his response would carry more weight than anyone else's. The therapist identified the pattern: she's outsourcing her self-worth to Jim the same way she outsourced it to her mother and Roy. "The pattern isn't 'who do I outsource to.' The pattern is 'I outsource.'"

The therapist asked: "What would your own verdict be? If Pam Beesly is the judge of Pam Beesly." Client delivered her first self-generated self-worth statement: "You're trying. You're painting at midnight. You're saying no. You're scared and you're doing it anyway. You're not where you want to be, but you're moving. You're becoming visible. And becoming visible is hard and it hurts and it's slow. But you're doing it. And that's enough. Not 'fine.' Not 'not good enough.' Enough."

She reported the experience as "peaceful. Not because it's perfect. But because it's mine. I said it. I decided. And I didn't ask anyone else if it was okay."

Objective

- Appearance: Neat; calm; grounded
- Behavior: Cooperative; produced the self-verdict without prompting; recognized the outsourcing pattern herself after the therapist's intervention
- Mood: Peaceful; "like the night parking lot"
- Affect: Genuine; quiet determination; no performed affect
- Speech: Clear; measured; deliberate pauses during the verdict
- Thought process: Linear; strong capacity for self-reflection and pattern identification
- Insight: Strong — can identify the outsourcing pattern (mother → Roy → Jim) and articulate her own verdict independently
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

The self-verdict is the Pam equivalent of Michael's "I deserved better" (session 14) — the first self-generated worth statement independent of external validation. The key distinction: Michael's was about the past (deserved better as a child); Pam's is about the present (she is enough right now, in process).

The outsourcing insight is the clinical core of the Jim material. The therapist correctly identified that Jim is not the problem — the pattern of outsourcing self-worth is. The client grasped this immediately and articulated it in her own words. The homework (practice the self-verdict) shifts the work from interpersonal to intrapersonal.

Plan

1. Homework: When "not good enough" arises, ask "What does Pam think?" Practice the self-verdict.
2. Next session: Session 13 — midpoint review, treatment plan assessment
3. The Jim feelings are acknowledged but not the focus. The focus is the outsourcing pattern.
4. Note: The self-verdict ("you're enough") is the culmination of the identity work (Goal 3). It will be reinforced through the remaining sessions.
5. Note: The "fine is a wall, distant is a window" formulation is the client's own cognitive defusion practice — she is generating ACT interventions without the therapist naming them as such.
