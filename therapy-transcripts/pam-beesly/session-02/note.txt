Date: Week 2
Clinician: Dr. James Pierce, PsyD
Client: Pam Beesly
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client completed the "fine compared to what" homework. She reported that the question arose repeatedly throughout the week and that most answers were "I don't know" — but a few produced concrete realizations, most notably that her weekends are entirely shaped by Roy's preferences and she cannot remember the last time she did something because she wanted to.

The session focused on art school and the "not good enough" narrative. Key disclosures:
- Client attended Pratt Institute for graphic design (a competitive program)
- After graduation, she did not apply for design jobs — took a receptionist position instead
- She stopped drawing because she "wasn't good enough" — but clarified that nobody told her this; she decided it herself
- The therapist identified the pattern as "pre-rejection" or "preemptive retreat" — she rejects herself before anyone else can reject her
- Client recognized this across multiple domains: art ("I fired myself before anyone else could fire me"), career ("I took the first thing available and called it temporary — three years ago"), and the relationship with Roy
- Key statement: "I've been so afraid of being rejected that I rejected myself out of my own life."

The therapist assigned homework: draw for 15 minutes. Client reported this as "more terrifying than coming to therapy" because drawing is "the thing I fired myself from."

Objective

- Appearance: Neat, casual; slightly more animated than session 1
- Behavior: Cooperative; more willing to sit with difficult material; still qualifying statements but less frequently
- Mood: "Both" — client's own framing, referring to the experience of holding competing truths simultaneously
- Affect: Constricted at start; genuine distress when discussing the "wrong life" realization; small laugh at the end (first humor in therapy)
- Speech: Soft; longer pauses than session 1; trail-offs diminishing as she becomes more direct
- Thought process: Linear; strong capacity for metaphor and self-reflection; the "preemptive retreat" formulation resonated immediately
- Insight: Developing — client can now see the pre-rejection pattern across multiple life domains; cannot yet see its origin
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

Session 2 identified the core defense mechanism: preemptive retreat / self-rejection. The client doesn't wait to be rejected — she rejects herself first and calls it "practical." This pattern is operating across at least three domains (art, career, relationship) and likely originates in family dynamics (to be explored in upcoming sessions).

The art school disclosure is clinically significant: attending Pratt indicates genuine talent and ambition. The fact that she stopped not because she failed but because she pre-decided she wasn't good enough — without external evidence — is the central clinical data point. The "preemptive retreat" formulation gives the client a framework for understanding a pattern she has been calling "maturity."

The homework (drawing) is both an assessment tool (can she return to something she abandoned?) and an exposure exercise (the act of drawing = the act of reaching for what she wants, which is the thing she's been avoiding).

Plan

1. Homework: Draw for 15 minutes. Not for anyone. Just for herself. Notice the resistance and what it feels like.
2. Next session: Session 3 — assessment wrap, present treatment plan, explore Roy more fully
3. The pre-rejection pattern is now the central clinical framework. Future sessions will trace it to its origin (family/mother) and test it through behavioral experiments.
4. Note: Client's first humor in therapy ("Thank you for not letting me say 'fine'") is a small alliance milestone.
