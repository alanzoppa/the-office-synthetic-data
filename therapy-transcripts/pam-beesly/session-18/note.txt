Session Type: Individual Therapy (Repair Consolidation & Roy Decision)

Subjective

Client reflected on the rupture and identified the most significant outcome: "Last week was the first time I've ever told someone they were wrong and stayed in the room." She distinguished the two responses she's always used — collapse or leave — and recognized she did neither. She named "staying" as potentially the most important moment in therapy.

She compared the therapist's repair behavior to her mother's ("my mom never owns it — she rewrites history") and Roy's ("he just goes quiet and the silence becomes the new normal"). The experience of someone owning their error was new and significant.

The therapist asked the core question: "If Pam Beesly defined Pam Beesly, what would change?" Client answered, quietly: "I'd leave Roy."

She then worked through the fear of being alone, using evidence from therapy: the museum trip alone, the midnight painting, the solo experience. "Being alone was where I was most myself. The fear of being alone is the old fear. The kid fear."

Key statement: "The Roy question is: can I be alone? And the answer is changing. I can. I went to a museum. I painted at midnight. I can be alone and it's not the worst thing. It's actually the best thing sometimes."

She then named the want: "I don't want Roy. I needed him. I needed someone there. But I didn't want him. And I'm saying that for the first time. I don't want Roy." She described this as "the biggest want I've ever named. A negative want. But still mine."

She described the experience as "a door I'm choosing. A door I'm walking through. Not yet. But I can see it."
