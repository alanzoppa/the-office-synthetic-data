Subjective

Client reported grief continuing as "weather" — waves that come and pass. "I thought grief would be like drowning. But it's more like rain. It falls and it's heavy and then it stops. And the ground is wet but the sky is clear. And I'm still standing."

The session focused on internalization — co-creating five coping phrases. All originated from the client's own words across prior sessions:

1. "My wants are not selfish." (from the mother's "selfish" voice, session 9)
2. "The voice that says 'not good enough' is not mine." (from the voice externalization, sessions 3-5)
3. "Reaching is not failing." (from the pre-rejection pattern, session 2)
4. "I am not boring. I am waiting to begin." (from the boring/invisible reframe, session 4)
5. "Thank you." (from the receiving practice, session 15)

Client plans to write these on index cards ("the Pam Beesly Emergency Kit") and keep them next to her paints. She described them as "the coat that fits, not the easygoing costume."

Assessment

The internalization session is the Pam equivalent of Michael's coping phrase session (23). Key difference: Michael's phrases are about the performance pattern ("I can be in this room without performing"); Pam's are about the suppression pattern ("My wants are not selfish"). Both sets are self-generated and portable.

The "thank you" phrase is the simplest and potentially the most powerful — it's the antidote to the lifetime pattern of deflecting compliments and making herself smaller. Two words. No costume.
