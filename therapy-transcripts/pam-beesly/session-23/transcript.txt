clinician: Good. Let's build them together. What would you say to yourself when the "not good enough" voice comes up?
client: "My wants are not selfish." That's the first one. Because the voice — my mother's voice — always said wanting was selfish. And it's not. Wanting is human. Wanting is what people do. My wants are not selfish.
clinician: Good. What about when you're about to pre-reject yourself? When you're about to not reach for something because you've already decided you'll fail?
client: "The voice that says 'not good enough' is not mine." Because it's hers. It's my mother's. And I can hear it now. I can hear it and I can say, "That's not me. That's her." And the hearing is enough. I don't have to make it go away. I just have to know it's not mine.
clinician: What about when you're about to avoid something? When the "don't reach, don't risk" pattern kicks in?
client: "Reaching is not failing." Because my whole life I thought reaching was the same as failing. If I reach and don't get it, that's failure. But reaching is just reaching. The reaching is the action. The outcome is separate. And the outcome doesn't determine whether the reaching was worth it. The reaching is always worth it. Even if I don't get the thing.
clinician: What about when you feel invisible? When the "background of the scene" feeling comes back?
client: "I am not boring. I am waiting to begin." Because boring was the label I gave myself when I was too scared to begin. And "waiting to begin" is more honest. It's not "boring." It's "not started yet." And "not started yet" means "still possible." And "still possible" is — that's hope. "Still possible" is hope.
clinician: One more. What about when someone gives you a compliment? When the instinct to shrink and deflect is strongest?
client: [pause] "I can want and survive disappointment." No — that's about wanting. For compliments... [pause] "I am worth being with. Even by myself." No. For compliments... [pause] "Thank you." Just "thank you." That's it. The whole phrase is "thank you." Not "thank you but I'm not that good." Not "thank you, it's just a sketch." Just "thank you." The two words. Without the deflection. Without the shrink. Just: "Thank you."
