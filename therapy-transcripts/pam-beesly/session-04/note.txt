Date: Week 4
Clinician: Dr. James Pierce, PsyD
Client: Pam Beesly
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client completed both drawings (office parking lot, coffee mug) and the "me or Mom" tracking. Key finding: "Every time the voice comes up and I ask 'Is this me or is this Mom?' — every single time, it's Mom. I haven't found a single critical thought that's actually mine." The critic has been fully externalized as her mother's voice.

The session explored what would happen if the voice was gone. Client initially said she'd be "a blank" — then corrected to "a canvas. But canvases are scary." The therapist noted that the voice, despite being cruel, provides a definition: "Not good enough" is a label, and labels organize behavior. Without the label, she'd have to figure out who she is — which is the terrifying part.

Key distinction introduced: boring vs. invisible. Client said "I'm boring." Therapist reframed: "Boring means you don't have anything inside. Invisible means you have things inside but you've learned not to show them." Client chose invisible: "I have things inside. I have the drawing. I have opinions. I have feelings. I just don't show them."

Core tension articulated: "I can be liked or I can be seen. And I chose liked. Because liked means people don't reject you. Liked means you get to stay. But you stay invisible."

Client then produced three "sharp" observations in rapid succession:
- "Easygoing is invisible in a nice dress."
- [re: the canvas] "Canvases are blank and whatever you put on them could be wrong."
- [re: being liked vs. seen] "I chose liked. I've always chosen liked."

She recognized these as her own voice — "louder, more there, more present" — distinct from her mother's "quiet disapproval." She noted that being seen and liked simultaneously was new: "You didn't look at me like I was too much."

Objective

- Behavior: Cooperative; produced spontaneous, sharp observations without prompting; visibly surprised by her own articulacy
- Mood: Energized; "good scared"

- Speech: Louder; more direct; fewer trail-offs; no "fine"
- Thought process: Linear; strong integrative capacity; the boring/invisible distinction was a breakthrough
- Insight: Strong — client can distinguish her own voice from her mother's and identify the liked/seen tension
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

Session 4 represents a significant shift. The "easygoing is invisible in a nice dress" formulation is the client's own clinical language — she is generating the therapeutic insights, not just receiving them. This confirms that the ACT approach (values clarification, defusion from the mother's voice) is working.

The liked/seen binary is the Pam equivalent of Michael's performing/connecting distinction: a binary the client has been living inside without knowing it. "I can be liked or I can be seen" is the belief that organizes her self-suppression. The session demonstrated that both can happen simultaneously — the client was seen (articulate, sharp, authentic) and liked (accepted by the therapist) in the same moment.
