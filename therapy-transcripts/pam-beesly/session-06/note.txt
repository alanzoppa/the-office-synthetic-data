Subjective

Client brought a want list with 11 items, including: "I want to paint, not just sketch," "I want to figure out who I am without Roy," "I want to be asked my opinion," "I want to say what I think without checking if it's okay first," "I want to be seen." She reported that once she started, the wants flowed "like a faucet."

The session focused on the Roy relationship (Goal 4). Key disclosures:
- Client distinguished "liking me" from "liking having me": "Liking me means he's curious. Liking having me means I'm there and that's enough."
- She identified the relationship as "liking having me" — she's a warm body, dinner-maker, wedding-planner. Roy doesn't know the real her.
- She's been hiding for 8 years from the person she's supposed to be closest to. "I've been invisible in my own home."
- The fear underneath the hiding: "If I show the real me and the real me isn't enough, then 'not good enough' isn't the voice. It's the truth." By never showing, she never confirms the fear.
- She articulated the deeper fear: "Hiding is safe and it's also a slow death. The real me is in there and she's getting smaller every year. One day she'll be so small that even I can't find her."
- The fear of being 70 and looking back: "I was invisible my whole life. And now it's too late."

Assessment

The Roy session is the Pam equivalent of Michael's Jan session (session 9). The central fear — that showing the real self will confirm the "not good enough" voice as truth — is the clinical core of the avoidance pattern. The client has been hiding to prevent confirmation, which is a variation of Michael's performing to prevent abandonment.

The "slow death" formulation is the client's own articulation of what the avoidance costs. It elevates the stakes beyond "I'm not happy" to "I'm losing myself." This is therapeutically useful: it creates urgency without crisis.

The clinician's intervention — "you showed the real you here and the room held" — uses the therapeutic relationship as evidence that being seen doesn't kill you. This is the same approach used with Michael (behavioral experiments as disconfirmation), adapted for Pam's avoidance pattern.
