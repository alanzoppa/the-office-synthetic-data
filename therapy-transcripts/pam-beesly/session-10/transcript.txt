clinician: You took up space. That's exactly what "no" does. It takes up space. And the room held.
client: The room held. And the thing I realized — I was managing Michael's feelings about no before he even had them. I was pre-managing. I was saying yes because I predicted he'd be upset by no. My mother's "are you sure?" when I was a kid taught me that no would be met with disapproval. So I pre-preempted the disapproval by never saying no. And Michael — Michael doesn't disapprove. He's too busy needing everyone to like him to disapprove of anyone.
clinician: You just identified something important. Michael needs everyone to like him. You need everyone to be comfortable. They're different strategies for the same fear: rejection.
client: They are. I never thought about it that way. Michael and I are — we're doing the same thing from opposite directions. He performs so people will like him. I manage so people won't be upset. Both of us are terrified of being rejected. Both of us are shaping ourselves around other people's needs. I just — I never saw myself in Michael before. He's so loud and I'm so quiet.
