Date: Week 19
Clinician: Dr. James Pierce, PsyD
Client: Pam Beesly
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported "holding the knowing" — the awareness that she doesn't want Roy. She described it as a stone in her chest that doesn't go away: "The knowing doesn't care where I'm standing. It's just true."

She described herself as standing in a doorway between two rooms: the Roy room (familiar, small, known) and the unknown room (big, new, uncertain). She is not moving but is "gathering" herself — "like standing at the edge of a pool and knowing you're going to jump."

Major development: client painted a self-portrait for the first time since high school. She deliberately painted honestly — shadows under eyes, downturned mouth — not the "nicer than I am" version her mother commented on. "I painted the real me. And honest is better than nice."

Homework: five actions for the next month:
1. Enroll in an art class
2. Show her work to someone new (beyond Oscar)
3. Tell her mother she's painting again
4. Have a real conversation with Roy
5. Think about what it would look like to leave

She identified the list as ordered by weight (art class lightest, leaving heaviest). She doesn't have to do all of them immediately — it's a month.

Objective

- Appearance: Neat; calm; deliberate
- Behavior: Cooperative; reflective; generated the action list independently; demonstrated capacity to hold a heavy truth without rushing
- Mood: "Gathering" — anticipatory, not crisis
- Affect: Genuine; quiet determination; no performed affect
- Speech: Clear; measured; quiet when arriving at the "leaving" item
- Thought process: Linear; strong planning capacity — actions ordered by weight, timeline specified
- Insight: Strong — can distinguish between knowing, gathering, and acting; understands she's in the gathering stage
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

The self-portrait is the Pam equivalent of Michael's mug reframe (session 16) — reclaiming a symbol that was previously corrupted by the mother's judgment. She painted honestly rather than nicely. "Honest is better than nice" is the Pam equivalent of Michael's "just is enough."

The action list represents the transition from awareness to the action phase of therapy. The five actions span all four treatment goals and provide a concrete roadmap for the remaining sessions. The "think about leaving" item is the heaviest but is framed as thinking, not doing — appropriate for the stage.

Plan

1. Homework: Begin working on the five-action list. No requirement to complete all five — just begin.
2. Next session: Session 20 — treatment plan update and identity work ("Who is Pam Beesly?")
3. The action list will be tracked across the remaining sessions
4. Note: The self-portrait is a clinical artifact — the client reclaimed the form her mother corrupted. It should be referenced in the grief session (22).
