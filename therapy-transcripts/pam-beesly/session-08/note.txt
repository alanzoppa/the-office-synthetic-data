Date: Week 8
Clinician: Dr. James Pierce, PsyD
Client: Pam Beesly
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client reported painting during lunch breaks in her car — a significant behavioral expansion. She described the "shrinking" at home in concrete terms: "From the front door to the living room, I go from me to easygoing Pam. In thirty seconds." The deflation is somatic and rapid.

The session built the avoidance map. Client identified avoidance across four domains:
1. **Work:** Says yes to everything; "no" feels like being difficult, and difficult is the opposite of easygoing
2. **Home:** Doesn't bring up anything that matters — art, books, films, feelings — because Roy's disinterest (10-second conversation, back to TV) is more painful than silence
3. **Mother:** Doesn't share anything real because of coded disapproval ("That's nice, honey. Are you sure you don't want to focus on the wedding?")
4. **Jim:** Pretends it's just friendship to avoid the disappointment of not being felt the same way about (or the complication of being felt the same way about)

Client synthesized the pattern: "Don't reach. Don't want. Don't risk. Stay small. Stay invisible. Stay safe." She recognized this is the operating system she's been running. She then identified the bottom of the fear: "If I'm not good enough, then I've been wrong my whole life. Not wrong about Roy or art — wrong about myself. Wrong about my own value."

The clinician reflected her behavioral evidence (painting twice, want list, Jim disclosure, pattern articulation). Client concluded: "That person isn't 'not good enough.' That person is brave. Maybe. A little." The "a little brave" formulation is hers — she claimed it.

Objective

- Appearance: Neat; more confident posture
- Behavior: Cooperative; direct; painting at lunch (behavioral activation); produced the avoidance map synthesis independently
- Mood: Scared but increasingly brave
- Affect: Genuine; fear when identifying the bottom of the pattern; pride when identifying herself as "a little brave"
- Speech: Clear; direct; no qualifying; no "fine"
- Thought process: Linear; strong synthesis — connected all four avoidance domains into one pattern
- Insight: Strong — can identify the core fear (being wrong about her own value) and counter it with behavioral evidence
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

The avoidance map session is the Pam equivalent of Michael's trigger map (session 6). The key difference: where Michael's triggers are about performance intensity (when does the show spike), Pam's are about suppression (when does she shut down). The four domains map cleanly to the treatment goals.

The "a little brave" formulation is the client's first positive self-label. It replaces "boring" (session 4) and "not good enough" (the mother's voice) with a self-generated, modest, genuine identity statement. "A little brave" is the Pam equivalent of Michael's "just human."

The homework (show Oscar one thing) is the first interpersonal behavioral experiment — the Pam equivalent of Michael's break room experiment. The therapist chose Oscar as safe (consistent with Michael's experience).

Plan

1. Homework: Show Oscar one thing she made. One "look at this." To someone safe. See what happens.
2. Next session: Session 9 — first behavioral experiment results; begin assertiveness work (Goal 3)
3. The "a little brave" label should be reinforced in future sessions
4. Note: The painting-at-lunch behavior is spontaneous — the client is generalizing the therapy work into daily life without being instructed. This is a strong prognostic sign.
