Date: Week 17
Clinician: Dr. James Pierce, PsyD
Client: Pam Beesly
Session Type: Individual Therapy (Rupture)

Subjective

Client arrived having painted three new pieces and written "I am an artist" on each. She reported the identity claim is settling ("week two of new shoes — still new, but my feet know where the ground is").

The therapist pushed: "Are you an artist who's hiding, or an artist who's showing?" noting that she paints at midnight, in her car, and only shows Oscar and the therapist. The intention was to challenge the private nature of the claim, but the timing was wrong — the client had just claimed the identity the previous week.

Client response was immediate and intense:
- Voice went cold and flat
- Explicitly compared therapist to her mother: "That sounds exactly like my mother. 'That's nice, honey, but are you sure?'"
- "You're taking the thing I just claimed and putting a question mark after it"
- "I just got here. I just said the words. And you're already moving the goalpost"
- "I thought this room was different. I thought you were different"
- Considered walking out ("I almost walked out of this room")

The therapist acknowledged the error: "I pushed you to claim the identity last week. And this week I pushed on how you're claiming it. That's too fast. I rushed it. I'm sorry." The therapist named the parallel: "I did the thing your mother does. I put a question mark after your achievement."

Client accepted the apology: "Thank you. That means something. That you said that. That you owned it." She then identified the assertiveness of her own response: "I told you off. And I didn't costume it. I didn't say 'I'm fine.' I said what I actually thought. And I didn't shrink."

Therapist noted: this was the most assertive she's been in the room — holding ground with the person whose opinion matters most to her.

Objective

- Appearance: Neat; closed body language during rupture; opened after repair
- Behavior: Cold, defended, direct during rupture; tearful but steady during repair; requested silence ("Can I just sit here?")
- Mood: Shifted from proud to betrayed to resolved
- Affect: Flat during rupture; genuine anger; tears during repair; relief after
- Speech: Clipped and direct during rupture; slower after repair
- Thought process: Rigid during rupture; the mother comparison was insightful despite distress
- Insight: Strong — identified the transference in real time ("that sounds like my mother")
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

This is the Pam equivalent of Michael's rupture (session 17). The therapist's timing error was pushing on the showing stage before the claiming stage had settled. The client experienced this as the mother's coded judgment — a question mark after an achievement.

The rupture and repair were clinically productive:
1. Client identified the transference independently ("sounds exactly like my mother")
2. Client held her ground with the therapist — the most assertive behavior in the room to date
3. The repair modeled that conflict doesn't end relationships — the Pam equivalent of Michael's repair session
4. The client's anger was validated as correct, not pathologized

Plan

1. Homework: Keep painting, keep writing "I am an artist." No new homework. Let the claim settle.
2. Next session: Session 18 — repair consolidation and the Roy question ("If you defined yourself, what would change?")
3. The rupture confirms: the client can now identify coded judgment in real time and respond assertively. The therapy is working.
4. Note: The therapist's apology is clinically significant — it's the Pam equivalent of Michael's therapist owning the timing error. Both therapists modeled that authority figures can be wrong and can own it.
