# Treatment Plan

**Client:** Pam Beesly
**Date:** Week 3 (Week of February 26, 2007)
**Clinician:** Dr. James Pierce, PsyD
**Session Type:** Individual Therapy — Treatment Plan Established

## Presenting Problem

Client self-referred for "feeling stuck." Assessment over sessions 1-3 reveals chronic self-suppression, avoidance of want, and a pattern of "preemptive retreat" — rejecting herself before anyone else can reject her. Client's self-worth is contingent on not disappointing others; she describes herself as "easygoing" and "nice," which assessment reveals functions as a defense against self-assertion. Client cannot identify what she wants, has abandoned her creative training (graphic design, Pratt Institute), and is in an 8-year relationship she describes as proximity rather than connection.

## Relevant History

- **Art/Career:** Attended Pratt Institute for graphic design. After graduation, did not apply for design jobs — took a receptionist position she describes as "temporary" (3 years ago). Stopped drawing because she "wasn't good enough" — no external rejection occurred; client pre-rejected herself.
- **Mother:** Pattern of soft disapproval disguised as support. "That's nice, honey" = "that's not very good." "Are you sure you want to do that?" = "don't do that." Client reports absorbing this voice so completely that she no longer hears it as her mother's — she experiences it as her own thought.
- **Relationship:** Engaged to Roy, together since age 19 (now 27). Roy is a warehouse worker at the same company. Client describes the relationship as "sitting next to someone on a bus — going to the same place but not together." Roy does not ask about her day, does not know about her art, and does not engage with her interests. Client cannot recall the relationship ever feeling different.
- **Occupational:** Receptionist at Dunder Mifflin Scranton. Describes herself as "the background of the scene." Qualifies the job as "not a bad job" unprompted.

## Mental Status (Session 3)

- **Appearance:** Neat, casual, modest; brought a drawing to session (first creative output shared in years)
- **Behavior:** Cooperative; more open than prior sessions; showed vulnerability by sharing artwork
- **Mood:** "Terrified but hopeful"
- **Affect:** Constricted at baseline; genuine warmth when artwork was validated; sadness when discussing mother
- **Speech:** Soft; fewer trail-offs than intake; more direct
- **Thought process:** Linear; strong capacity for metaphor and self-reflection
- **Insight:** Developing — can externalize the critic as mother's voice; can distinguish between drawing and grading
- **Safety:** Denies SI/HI

## Diagnoses

- **Generalized Anxiety Disorder** (provisional) — chronic worry about disappointing others, anticipatory anxiety about self-assertion, somatic anxiety (cold hands, shaking) during novel situations
- **Rule out: Persistent Depressive Disorder** (deferred pending further assessment) — chronic low-grade dissatisfaction, self-described as "stuck," anhedonia around creative pursuit

## Core Cognitive Schema

**"If I reach for what I actually want, I'll be disappointed — so it's safer to not want and not reach."**

Origin: Mother's soft disapproval (wanting = selfish, reaching = dangerous) → Internalized as "not good enough" voice → Applied to art (stopped drawing), career (took first available job), relationship (stayed with Roy to avoid the risk of wanting more)

Current manifestation: Pre-rejection ("I reject myself before anyone else can reject me"); minimizing ("fine" as a defense); self-suppression (shaping herself around others' assumed expectations); inability to identify personal wants; assertiveness avoidance (saying yes to everything to avoid the danger of no).

## Treatment Goals

| # | Goal | Objectives | Target Session |
|---|------|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | Learn to identify personal wants independent of others | Values clarification exercises; distinguish "what I want" from "what they want"; practice naming desires in session | Ongoing from session 3 |
| 2 | Challenge the "not good enough" voice | Identify voice as mother's, not own; practice cognitive defusion; track when the voice activates and what triggers it | Sessions 5-10 |
| 3 | Practice asserting self | Behavioral experiments: say no once per week; ask for one thing per week; express preferences in low-stakes situations | Sessions 9-15 |
| 4 | Understand the Roy relationship | Explore choice vs. default; identify what the relationship provides and what it costs; connect to the avoidance pattern | Sessions 14-20 |

## Modality

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) with:
- **Values clarification:** What matters to Pam, not what she's been told should matter
- **Cognitive defusion:** Separating from the "not good enough" voice
- **Behavioral activation:** Small actions toward what she wants (drawing, asserting, choosing)
- **Acceptance:** Learning to tolerate the discomfort of wanting and the risk of disappointment

## Frequency

Weekly, 50 minutes

## Review Date

Session 13 — review progress on all four goals, adjust objectives, and determine whether the plan needs revision

## Homework (Session 3 → 4)

Draw for 15 minutes, twice this week. When "not good enough" arises, ask: "Is this me or is this Mom?" Just notice the question. No need to answer.
