Date: Week 7
Clinician: Dr. James Pierce, PsyD
Client: Pam Beesly
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client painted for the second time — a watercolor of the parking lot. Color mixing issues (practice) but the therapist noted the light handling as competent. Client caught the mother's voice ("this is amateur") and continued painting. She reported painting felt like "breaking a rule."

Jim appeared in session for the first time. Client described him as someone who "notices things" — he asked about her drawing, which no one else at the office knows about. She reported feeling "like the main character" when talking to Jim versus "the background" with Roy. She described a physical sensation: tightness in her chest when she goes home after a day of being seen at work.

Key disclosure: "I think I have feelings for Jim. I've had them for a while. I didn't let myself know that." She framed Jim as "the catalyst making it impossible to keep hiding." She explicitly stated she should not act on the Jim feelings until she resolves the Roy question — "otherwise I'm just jumping from one person's definition to another." Therapist affirmed this as wise.

Client articulated her identity development: "I'm still a sketch, not a painting. She's the outline without the color." She identified the gap between her work-self (bigger, painting, wanting) and her home-self (shrinking, invisible, easygoing). "The real me is bigger at work now. And she goes home and she shrinks. Every night. And the shrinking hurts."

Objective

- Appearance: Neat; brought the watercolor painting
- Behavior: Cooperative; disclosed feelings for Jim voluntarily; showed emotional maturity in sequencing (Roy before Jim)
- Mood: Scared but honest
- Affect: Genuine; warmth discussing Jim; pain discussing the shrinking at home; relief after disclosing feelings
- Speech: Clear; direct; paused meaningfully when arriving at the Jim disclosure
- Thought process: Linear; strong capacity for self-regulation — identifying the right sequence of work without prompting
- Insight: Strong — can see that Jim is a catalyst, not a solution; can distinguish between "Roy's Pam" and "Jim's Pam" and recognize that "Pam's Pam" is the goal
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

The Jim disclosure is the Pam equivalent of Michael's Jan exploration — the live relational material that forces the core pattern into the open. The client's instinct to resolve Roy before pursuing Jim is clinically sound and demonstrates that the therapy is working: she's not jumping from one definition to another; she's trying to find her own.

The "sketch, not a painting" self-description is the client's own metaphor for her identity development. She recognizes she's in process — not complete, but no longer blank. This is progress from session 4 ("a blank canvas") to session 7 ("the outline without the color").

The shrinking-at-home observation gives the therapy concrete somatic data: the cost of the suppression pattern is physical (chest tightness), not just emotional.

Plan

1. Homework: Keep painting. Notice the gap between work-self and home-self. No action required — just observe the shrinking.
2. Next session: Session 8 — the avoidance map (when does she avoid, and what triggers it?)
3. The Jim feelings are acknowledged but parked. The therapeutic sequence is: self-definition → Roy decision → (possibly) Jim. This is the client's own formulation.
4. Note: The "wise" comment from the therapist is significant — it's the first time Pam has been called something other than "nice" or "easygoing." She noticed it.
