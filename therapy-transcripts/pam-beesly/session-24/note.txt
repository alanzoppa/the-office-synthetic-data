Subjective

Client used all three coping cards during the week:
1. "My wants are not selfish" — used at art supply store to justify buying real watercolor paper (first time using professional materials). "Real watercolor paper is a completely different experience. The paper was a want. And the want was not selfish."
2. "Thank you" — used at art class when Maria said her kitchen painting was the strongest in the class. Client said "Thank you, Maria" without deflection or shrinking. "The instinct to shrink is like gravity. But I didn't shrink."
3. "The voice that says 'not good enough' is not mine" — used during a phone call with her mother. Mother said "Are you sure you should be spending money on that?" Client responded: "Mom, the paper matters to me." Mother went quiet. Said "Okay, Pam." Client repeated: "It matters to me." Mother said "okay" again. Client reported feeling "three inches taller."

When told next session is the last, client reported: "Scared. Grateful. Sad. Ready. All of it." She described herself as "wet watercolor ready. Still moving. Still becoming." She plans to bring a painting to the final session.

Pre-emptive gratitude offered: "Thank you for the questions. For not putting a question mark after me. For sitting there while I said 'I'm an artist.' For owning your mistake. For letting me be angry. For letting me grieve. For letting me paint. For not making me nice. For being the room where the coat fit."
