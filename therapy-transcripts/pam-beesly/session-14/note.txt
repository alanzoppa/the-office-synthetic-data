Date: Week 14
Clinician: Dr. James Pierce, PsyD
Client: Pam Beesly
Session Type: Individual Therapy

Subjective

Client went to the museum alone (first solo action toward a want). She spent three hours looking at paintings and cried in front of a Mary Cassatt — a painting of a mother looking at a child with total attention. She thought: "Nobody has ever looked at me like that." She cried in public for the first time and didn't hide it. Described feeling "clean" afterward.

The session focused on anger at Roy (first anger expression). Key disclosures:
- Wedding planning memory: Roy's only contribution to his own wedding was "What time do I need to be there." Client planned everything alone and called it "fine" while being "furious for a year."
- Anger at Roy: "I'm angry that he doesn't ask about my day. I'm angry that he doesn't know I paint. I'm angry that I paint at midnight when he's asleep. I'm angry that I'm hiding in my own house."
- Anger at self: "I made myself invisible. I shaped myself into the easygoing fiancée who doesn't need anything. I made myself into furniture. And then I was angry that he treated me like furniture. But I built the furniture."
- Key insight: "The thing I was afraid of — not being seen — I made it happen by hiding. The fear created the reality."
- She recognized: "I have the right to be seen. I have the right to be known. And I've been giving that right away. And now I want it back."

Objective

- Appearance: Neat; more animated than usual
- Behavior: Cooperative; expressed anger for the first time; cried in public (museum); demonstrated capacity to hold anger without it becoming rage
- Mood: Angry; "angry and allowed"
- Affect: Genuine; steady, hot anger; determination
- Speech: Clear; direct; louder; no qualifiers
- Thought process: Linear; strong integrative capacity — connected the fear to the outcome (fear created the reality)
- Insight: Strong — can identify that the avoidance pattern created the very thing it was protecting against
- Safety: Denies SI/HI

Assessment

This is the Pam equivalent of Michael's anger session (14). Key difference: Michael's anger was at others (father, stepfather, Jan, self); Pam's anger is at Roy and at herself for creating the invisibility. The insight — "the fear created the reality" — is a clinical milestone. She recognizes that the avoidance pattern is self-perpetuating, not just self-protective.

The museum trip is the first action-oriented behavioral experiment. She went alone, without managing anyone, and had a genuine emotional experience in public. The museum cry is the Pam equivalent of Michael's grief session — the first time she lets herself feel something she was told she shouldn't feel (anger, in this case seeded by the museum grief).

Plan

1. Homework: Let anger exist this week. Don't push it down, costume it, or make it nice. Hear what it's telling you matters (being seen).
2. Next session: Session 15 — anger at her mother (connecting the "don't fuss" and "don't be selfish" to the suppression pattern)
3. The museum trip is a prototype — the client can act on wants independently and survive the experience
4. Note: The "I have the right to be seen" statement is the Pam equivalent of Michael's "I deserved better" — first claim of rights
